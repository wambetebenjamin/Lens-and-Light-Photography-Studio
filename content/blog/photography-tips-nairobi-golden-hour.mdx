Golden hour in Nairobi moves fast — you have roughly 25 usable minutes once
the sun drops below the acacia line. Here is exactly what we set before we
even get out of the car.

## 1. Shoot in Aperture Priority, not full Manual

In changing light, full Manual mode costs you shots while you chase the
exposure triangle. We set Aperture Priority at **f/2.2–f/2.8**, let the
camera handle shutter speed, and ride exposure compensation with a thumb
dial instead.

## 2. Keep ISO flexible

Don't lock ISO to 100 "for quality." As the sun drops, let ISO float up to
800–1600. A little clean noise beats a blurry, underexposed memory every
time.

## 3. White balance: Cloudy, always

Auto white balance fights the warm tones that make golden hour special.
Setting white balance to **Cloudy** (around 6500K) exaggerates the gold
rather than correcting it away.

## 4. Backlight, then fill

Position your subject with the sun behind them for that signature rim-light
glow, then use a reflector — or even a white kanga cloth — to bounce light
back into their face.

## 5. Focus on the eyes, recompose fast

Wide apertures mean paper-thin depth of field. We use single-point
autofocus locked to the nearest eye, then recompose in under a second to
avoid focus drift.

None of this replaces an experienced eye behind the camera — but it's the
technical foundation every session at Lens & Light starts from.
